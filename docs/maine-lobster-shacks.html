Pounds, shacks and wharves

A lobster pound began as a saltwater pen for keeping lobsters alive. Today the name usually means a waterfront place that cooks them to order, in big pots of seawater or over a wood fire, with picnic tables outside; a lobster shack is the takeout version. The Maine lobster roll is usually served cold, the meat lightly dressed with mayonnaise in a toasted, buttered split-top bun, and many places will make it with warm butter instead.

Lobster shacks & rolls: questions

Are Maine lobster shacks open all year?

Not all of them. 26 of the 37 here are seasonal and 11 aren't marked seasonal. 19 of the seasonal ones post their dates, and we list them with each place. A place that's closed for the winter hasn't closed for good, so check its season before you drive.

Lobster pound or lobster shack: what's the difference?

Mostly the setting. A pound keeps live lobsters, often in tanks or a saltwater pen, and cooks them to order for a lobster dinner: the lobster, corn, steamers, butter. A shack is a takeout counter, usually for lobster rolls, chowder and fried seafood. Of the 37 places here, 27 are pounds or shacks and 24 list a lobster roll.

Cold with mayo or warm with butter?

In Maine the lobster roll is traditionally cold: lobster meat with a little mayonnaise in a toasted, buttered split-top bun. Warm meat with melted butter is often called Connecticut style. Many places serve both or ask which you want; where a place told us, it's in the “Known for” line.

Which lobster place here has been at its address the longest?

Of the 13 places on this list with a checked year at the same address, the longest-running is Fox's Lobster House in York Beach (York), there since 1936. Places without a checked year may be older, so this answers for our list, not for the whole state. Maine's oldest restaurants has the 22 places with 40 or more years at the same address.
