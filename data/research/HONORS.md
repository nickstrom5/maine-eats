# Maine restaurant honors (checked 2026-10-08)

Sources: the James Beard Foundation's own winner and nominee pages on jamesbeard.org, cross-checked against JBF's past-awards
database (which loads client-side, so it isn't cited) and Portland Food Map's Maine list, which matched it on every Maine winner.
Wikipedia's JBF award lists were used for the pre-2015 years. JBF's word "Nominee" means **finalist**. Records are in
`honors.json` (open, 10 places) and `closed_honors.json` (closed, 3 places). Semifinalists are left out.

## MICHELIN

**Maine has no MICHELIN Guide restaurant selection.** Michelin's own press release of Aug. 11, 2026
([michelinmedia.com](https://michelinmedia.com/pages/blog/detail.aspx?CatID=0&ArticleID=1479)) lists every North American guide:
New York, Chicago, D.C., California, Florida, Toronto, Vancouver, Colorado, Atlanta, Mexico, Texas, Québec, the American South,
Boston, Philadelphia, the Southwest and the American Great Lakes (2027). Maine isn't on it. The 2026 Northeast Cities edition covers
only Boston, Chicago, New York, Philadelphia and Washington, D.C. A 2023 Portland Food Map post
([link](https://www.portlandfoodmap.com/michelin-in-maine/)) reports only a writer's prediction that Maine could be next. The
"July 2026 Michelin hotels article" in our notes wasn't checked, because it is on guide.michelin.com, which we don't fetch.
No Michelin honors were added.

## Corrections to our September notes

1. **Waterman's Beach Lobster closed for good on Sept. 4, 2016.** The 2001 America's Classics award is correct. It is in the closed
   file.
2. **Bagaduce Lunch:** JBF files it under Brooksville, but Portland Food Map and Wikipedia place it in **Penobscot**. With 10 searches
   and three news archives checked, no 2025–26 source shows it open. It is in neither file and is a LEADS candidate.
3. **Rob Evans won in 2009**, which matches our notes. The Bangor Daily News and Portland Food Map 2022 stories say 2008, which is
   wrong: 2008 was a finalist year. Hugo's is closed. Duckfat opened in 2005 while Evans still owned Hugo's, so "later ran Duckfat"
   is loose. The award stays with Hugo's.
4. **Arrows did not close in 2018.** It was listed for sale in October 2013, was "the former Arrows" by 2014, and became The
   Velveteen Habit, which has also closed. In June 2026 the building (41 Ogunquit Road, York/Cape Neddick) was leased for a new
   restaurant, Mazon. **Frasier and Gaier now:** no 2025–26 source shows them running a Maine restaurant. The 2013 BDN story had
   them at MC Perkins Cove, but an undated Visit Maine page (a blocked host, so not cited) names other co-owners there.
5. **Melissa Kelly:** 2013 is correct. Primo's site also mentions her 1999 Best Chef: Northeast, but JBF ties that award to Old
   Chatham Sheepherding Co. Inn in New York, so it is not on Primo's record.
6. **Taylor and Wiley (2017):** JBF ties the award to **Eventide Oyster Co.** only, not to Hugo's or Big Tree Hospitality.
7. **The Quarry:** confirmed as the **winner** of Outstanding Hospitality in **2023**. At that year's ceremony the category was
   called "Outstanding Hospitality Presented by American Airlines."
8. **ZU Bakery and Atsuko Fujimoto (2024) were two separate wins.** ZU Bakery, Barak Olins's bakery, won **Outstanding Bakery**.
   Atsuko Fujimoto of **Norimoto Bakery** won **Outstanding Pastry Chef or Baker**. Fujimoto is not part of ZU.
9. **Dana Street (2026 Outstanding Restaurateur):** confirmed. JBF lists "Fore Street, Scales, Standard Baking Co., and others."
   Street & Co. is named by Portland Food Map, not by JBF. JBF's database gives his town as Yarmouth, but the winners page says
   Portland.
10. **Missing from our notes:** Rob Tod of Allagash won in 2019 (a brewery, so not added).
